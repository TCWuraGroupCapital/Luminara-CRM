# Designing agent-native CRMs that outlast 2030

Research date: 15 November 2026. Not legal advice.

An AI-agent-native CRM feels human-crafted when its chrome gets quieter and its evidence gets louder: restrained, tokenised visual design, a stable and conventional shell, and an agent that appears as a named, accountable actor with receipts, not as a sparkle, orb or chat persona. The generic "AI slop" look is a convergence problem (models and templates default to the same safe choices), so the durable counter-move is deliberate choice, not a different trend ([Superdesign](https://www.superdesign.dev/blog/why-ai-design-looks-generic)). The strongest evidence for what lasts comes from legibility failures: Apple's Liquid Glass drew expert criticism and a user-facing opacity toggle within about four months ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/20/apple-will-let-users-roll-back-the-liquid-glass-look-with-new-tinted-option)). Forecasters agree that users will spend less time operating interfaces and more time supervising agents, which moves the centre of a CRM from forms to review, diff, receipt and audit views ([NN/g State of UX 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-of-ux-2026/)). Regulation points the same way: the EU AI Act's Art. 50(1) chatbot disclosure has applied since 2 August 2026, and Australia's automated-decision transparency duty starts 10 December 2026. For Luminara, the design that survives is a graded-autonomy ladder (create and move-one act directly; delete and move-many wait for approval) backed by immutable receipts, a re-check before any approved call runs, and a visible, machine-readable AI provenance on everything the agent touches. Much of the supporting evidence is practitioner guidance and vendor documentation rather than controlled studies, and where this report goes beyond its sources it says so.

## The 2024–2026 "AI dashboard" look is an unchosen default, and the best studios subtract

Critics converge on a short list of tells: purple or indigo-to-blue gradients, glass panels and glow, Inter by default, identical icon-heading-text card grids, rounded-everything, sparkle icons and bento sprawl ([Impeccable catalogue](https://impeccable.style/slop)). That catalogue is vendor material, but a second source supplies the mechanism: safe design choices "that work universally and offend no one" dominate training data, the indigo accent traces to a Tailwind default, and coding agents avoid tilted, glass or 3D directions as harder to implement ([Superdesign, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.superdesign.dev/blog/why-ai-design-looks-generic)). The implication is that no single style is the offender. The tell is the absence of decisions, and adopting a different fashionable font does not fix it, since the same catalogue lists Geist alongside Inter as an overused default. Two of the specific tells in the brief (gradient chat bubbles and "Good morning, Name!" empty states) had no primary citation and are treated here as team heuristics.

The sparkle icon has the best evidence against it. In an NN/g study of 107 participants, none linked sparkles to artificial intelligence; the top associations were favouriting or saving and visual effects or optimisation (16.82% each), and 73% associate a star with saving ([NN/g, Sep 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-sparkles-icon-problem/)). NN/g recommends always pairing the icon with a label and preferring established icons. Shape of AI documents the alternative: function-specific symbols (pencil for edit, looped arrows for remix, quote mark for summarise), on the argument that AI-native products need not rely on magic imagery ([Shape of AI](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/symbols)). Bento grids are in a similar position: critics say they present "a tray of facts" instead of an argument, while defenders call them a scanning aid, all at blog level ([Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/insight/10-trends-creatives-are-so-over-in-2026/); [SaaSFrame, defending](https://www.saasframe.io/blog/designing-bento-grids-that-actually-work-a-2026-practical-guide)). A CRM has a natural defence: its cells hold real, different records.

What leading studios do instead is documented most concretely by Linear. Its March 2024 redesign aimed to "reduce visual noise, maintain visual alignment, and increase the hierarchy and density of navigation elements", moved headings to Inter Display, and replaced a 98-variable theme with three inputs (base colour, accent, contrast) generated in LCH so light and dark share one perceptually uniform generator ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Its 12 March 2026 refresh went further: principles of "Don't compete for attention you haven't earned" and "Structure should be felt not seen", a dimmer sidebar so content dominates, fewer and smaller icons, softened or removed separators, and warmer, less saturated greys ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). On iOS, Linear built its own glass material and skipped refraction because "refraction can make dense professional interfaces harder to read" ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/linear-liquid-glass)). Linear's moves are the exact inverse of the slop list. Granola shows the same restraint for AI specifically: co-founder Sam Stephenson's stated principle is "Be calming", with no visible meeting bot (reported via a podcast summary, quotes not verified on the page; [Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/calm-ai-for-crazy-days-inside-granola-s-design-philosophy-with-co-founder-sam-stephenson/)). Evidence is thin for other admired products (Attio's reported ~36px rows and ~14px Inter come only from unofficial reverse-engineering, [nadesign](https://nadesign.pages.dev/references/attio-layout-patterns/)), so the report does not lean on them. Finally, the long-running critique that dashboard mockups omit empty states and long strings dates from 2013 ([Intercom](https://intercom.com/blog/the-dribbblisation-of-design)) and is secondary for 2024–2026, but it still gives a cheap test: design every screen with empty, loading, error, nine-digit and very long data.

## Agentic software needs a graded-autonomy ladder, and receipts that carry the evidence

Across products, the converging pattern is a ladder: reads and drafts are free; writes, sends and deletes are gated; a user or admin controls the dial. Notion Agent offers three modes: "Always ask", a default "Review for me" where the agent self-reviews and pauses on actions it flags unsafe such as deleting pages or sending email, and "Skip all approvals"; Notion itself warns that the model-judged mode "relies on the agent's review" and may not flag what the user would, and admins can disable skip-all ([Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/choose-how-notion-agent-asks-for-your-approval)). Attio's Ask Attio "will show you exactly what it's about to do and wait for your go-ahead" ([Attio](https://attio.com/changelog/2026/ask-attio-to-take-action)). HubSpot's Agent Inbox filters runs by status (Needs approval, Success, Running, Failed, Waiting) and shows trigger origin, output, expandable thinking steps and sources, with refinements stacking beneath earlier output ([HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/ai/review-agent-output)). Superhuman never auto-sends: auto drafts land in the drafts folder, and editing one converts it to an ordinary draft ([Superhuman](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46183279736461-Superhuman-Mail-for-Gmail), secondary summary). Luminara's rule (direct create and single move; approval for delete and multi-move) is a deterministic gate, which the sources favour over a model-judged one. The Notion warning supports that choice, and the notes' own inference is to pair any model-judged gate with deterministic rules on object class.

Friction should track reversibility, not agent confidence. Smashing's February 2026 pattern set (Intent Preview, Autonomy Dial, Explainable Rationale, Confidence Signal, Action Audit & Undo, Escalation Pathway) calls an intent preview "non-negotiable" for irreversible, financial or data-sharing actions, and suggests four dial levels per task type ([Smashing](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-agentic-ai-practical-ux-patterns/)). Its numeric thresholds (for example, reverting more than 5% of a task's actions should disable automation for it) are illustrative, not empirical, but they are directly implementable as telemetry. Shape of AI's verification pattern adds two cautions that matter for a CRM: if users can switch confirmation off, make the skipped verification visible (its example shows a red alert when "Always Ask" is disabled), and alert users out-of-band so asynchronous runs do not stall ([Shape of AI](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/verification)). There is no controlled evidence on approval fatigue or automation bias for agent UIs in these notes; Art. 14 of the AI Act at least names automation bias as something overseers must stay aware of ([Art. 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)). The practical defence is to make the approval screen show the diff, not just an "Approve" button, and to keep approvals rare by letting low-risk work run directly.

Provenance is best done without iconography. Granola shows user-typed notes in black and AI-added text in gray, and editing gray text turns it black, so ownership transfers with the human edit; AI bullets link back to the transcript moment ([Granola help](https://help.granola.ai/article/ai-enhanced-notes), secondary summary). Linear models agents as app users that appear in assignee menus with their own activity pages; a delegated issue keeps the human as the responsible assignee ([Linear agents](https://linear.app/docs/agents-in-linear.md)), and its agent-interaction API distinguishes thought, elicitation, action, response and error activities, shows session states including awaitingInput and stale, and forbids agents from fabricating user prompts ([Linear developers](https://linear.app/developers/agent-interaction)). GitHub treats the pull request as the receipt: session logs show tools used, rulesets can block the agent, and the docs say logs "do not replace your own review" ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/agents/coding-agent/about-coding-agent)). Salesforce records every turn, LLM call, action invocation and guardrail check in session traces exportable over OpenTelemetry (beta) ([Salesforce](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/ai/agentforce/guide/otel-api.html)). Undo is weaker than any of this suggests. Products mostly rely on version history or an expiring undo window, and Notion states plainly that the undo path depends on where the mistake happened and may mean fixing it in the connected app ([Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/choose-how-notion-agent-asks-for-your-approval)); no product found offers true rollback of external side effects. A further gap is that Attio, Folk, Day.ai and Lightfield documentation on provenance and undo was not found, so claims about CRM competitors here are limited.

Luminara's receipt design is stronger than most public examples on one point: it re-checks before an approved call runs. A proposal approved minutes or hours after it was requested can target records that have since changed owner, stage or existence. Storing the records' state at request time makes this tractable, because the approval screen can show "as requested" against "now" and the executor can refuse or re-propose if they differ. This is the classic time-of-check to time-of-use gap, and none of the researched products documents handling it; treat it as Luminara's differentiator and test it explicitly. One honest-labelling point follows from the receipt fields: the agent's reason is unverified model text. Display it as a claim ("Agent says") in a visually distinct, secondary style, never as a fact or system reason, and keep it out of any control path. Smashing's rationale pattern places "why I did this" beside Undo, which fits, provided the label makes the unverified status clear. Finally, undo for agent-created leads needs a deliberate decision: reversing a creation is a delete, which the policy gates. The cleanest reading (an inference, not sourced) is a short, visible undo window for the creating user on agent-created records, recorded as its own receipt, with everything else under approval.

## Through 2030, durable traits are conventions, user-controlled contrast and command surfaces, not effects

NN/g's central warning on generative UI is that "constantly changing UIs will cause usability problems", because users speed up on frequently used tools and understanding depends on conventions; its upside case is accessibility, such as auto-adjusted font size and contrast ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/generative-ui/)). The products are real: Google Research's generative UI renders model-authored interfaces in Gemini and Search AI Mode, with expert-designed sites preferred first and generation sometimes taking a minute or more ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/), via summary); MCP Apps became the first official MCP extension on 26 January 2026, rendering tool-supplied `ui://` resources in sandboxed iframes where hosts can audit messages and require approval for UI-initiated tool calls ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-01-26-mcp-apps/)). The durable form for a CRM is constrained generation inside a stable shell: record pages, navigation and action bars deterministic, with ephemeral generated panels only for one-off analyses and confirmations. That conclusion is inference; no longitudinal study of generative UI in B2B tools exists in the notes.

On materials, Liquid Glass is the instructive case. Apple describes it as translucent, reflecting and refracting its surroundings ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/)); NN/g's expert review found text unreadable over busy backgrounds, shrunken tap targets and unstable navigation, and said "Motion for motion's sake is not usability" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)). Apple then added a Tinted option in iOS 26.1 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/20/apple-will-let-users-roll-back-the-liquid-glass-look-with-new-tinted-option)). NN/g's web guidance for glassmorphism is to check contrast against sampled complex backgrounds, prefer backgrounds you control, and offer a solid fallback ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/glassmorphism/)). The transferable lesson is that the control for legibility is the durable layer and the glass is decoration. Spatial interfaces are a niche: visionOS 26 added persistent widgets and enterprise APIs, and enterprise use is reported stronger than consumer, but sales figures are unverified ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/09/15/apple-releases-visionos-26/)), so a spatial CRM surface through 2030 is a bet this evidence does not support.

Ambient and calm design fits agents if the default is silence. Weiser and Seely Brown introduced calm technology in 1995 and Amber Case formalised principles in 2015: minimise attention demands, use the periphery ([Case Organic](https://www.caseorganic.com/post/principles-of-calm-technology)). NN/g calls 2026 "the year of AI fatigue" and says winners treat AI as a background tool, not a headline feature; it also notes standardised design systems make UI cheap, so taste and judgement differentiate ([NN/g State of UX 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-of-ux-2026/)). The implied agent surface is silent work, a digest with provenance, and interruptions only for approvals or failures. The failure mode to design against is the silent stall: Claude Code's background sessions stall when login expires ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/iam)). Command palettes have the strongest durability evidence: Superhuman describes its palette as teaching shortcuts and being the single place to find every command ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-to-build-a-remarkable-command-palette/)). Natural language is best added as a fallback inside that palette with a preview step, an inference. Evidence on voice in B2B desktop software was not found, so the report makes no claim. Gartner's mixed signals argue for modest autonomy: it forecasts that 33% of enterprise software will include agentic AI by 2028, yet over 40% of agentic projects will be cancelled by end-2027 ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)). For type and colour, the verified, implementable trend is perceptual colour: OKLCH has been widely available since May 2023 ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/oklch)), and Linear's three-input theme generator is the model to copy. Typeface fashion has no primary evidence in the notes, so none is prescribed beyond avoiding unchosen defaults.

## Accessibility and law converge on visible AI labels, operable controls and records

Under the EU AI Act, Art. 50(1) requires providers to design systems that interact directly with people so that those people are told they are dealing with AI, unless obvious to a reasonably well-informed observer, and Art. 50(5) requires the information to be clear, distinguishable, given at the latest at first interaction and to meet applicable accessibility requirements ([Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) left Art. 50(1) untouched; only the Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking duty got a grace period, to 2 December 2026, and only for generative systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act); [McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/data-privacy-and-cyber-risk/ai-transparency-european-commissions-guidelines-on-article-50-part-1-provider-obligations)). These dates come from law-firm summaries, not EUR-Lex. The non-binding Commission guidelines (20 July 2026) say disclosure buried in terms and conditions is insufficient, standardised labels are recommended, and the "obvious" exception is narrow ([McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/data-privacy-and-cyber-risk/ai-transparency-european-commissions-guidelines-on-article-50-part-1-provider-obligations)). A voluntary Code of Practice on marking and labelling, published 10 June 2026, offers an optional common EU icon set ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content)). The Art. 50(4) deployer label covers only text published to inform the public on public-interest matters, so ordinary CRM notes and emails are probably out of scope, though whether assistant-drafted B2B email falls under 50(2) is unresolved in the sources. A cheap, safe design is a visible AI badge plus machine-readable provenance fields on every agent-touched record.

A general CRM is unlikely to be high-risk, since Annex III lists recruitment, performance monitoring, credit scoring and life or health insurance pricing and does not list lead scoring; high-risk dates moved to 2 December 2027 for Annex III ([Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/); [Art. 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)). Art. 14 remains the best checklist for the UI: understand limits, stay aware of automation bias, interpret output, override or reverse it, and halt via a "stop" button. On automated decisions about individuals, CJEU case law reads broadly: SCHUFA held a score can itself be a decision when it plays a determining role, and Dun & Bradstreet requires explaining the procedure and principles applied ([Clyde & Co](https://clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/03/ecj-ruling-on-automated-decision-making-and-data-s)). Australia adds a transparency duty from 10 December 2026: APP entities must describe in privacy policies the kinds of personal information and decisions involved when a computer program substantially and directly informs a decision that could significantly affect an individual. The OAIC reads "computer program" broadly (rule-based and generative tools included) and says human review does not exempt a tool that materially informs the decision, but it creates no right to contest ([Allens](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2026/10/mandatory-automated-decision-making-disclosure-requirements-are-coming-key-takeaways-from-the-oaic-guidance/)). Australia's voluntary Guidance for AI Adoption (October 2025) includes "maintain human control" among six practices ([industry.gov.au](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/guidance-ai-adoption)). Luminara's lead create, move and delete actions are unlikely to be significant decisions about individuals by themselves, but a feature that prioritises or scores leads for differential offers could be, so the receipt store doubles as raw material for a customer-facing automated-decision register (an inference). The sources did not cover Australia's Disability Discrimination Act position on WCAG, so none is claimed.

On accessibility standards, WCAG 2.2 (5 October 2023) adds criteria that bite on agent panels: 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured, 2.5.7 Dragging Movements, 2.5.8 Target Size (24x24 CSS px minimum), 3.2.6 Consistent Help, 3.3.7 Redundant Entry and 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). Status messages (4.1.3, from WCAG 2.1) cover streamed replies and "needs approval" notices; the notes cite this from knowledge, not a fetched page. WCAG 3.0 remains a Working Draft (10 September 2026) whose text-contrast algorithm is "yet to be determined"; APCA was dropped from the 2023 draft and the draft is not expected to be final before about 2030, so WCAG 2.2 stays the compliance reference ([W3C WCAG 3](https://w3.org/TR/WCAG3/); [Adrian Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2026/04/wcag3-contrast-as-of-april-2026.html)). The sensible approach is to meet WCAG 2 ratios (4.5:1 text, 3:1 UI) as the pass/fail and use APCA Lc only as an advisory cross-check, especially for dark mode and thin type. The European Accessibility Act targets consumer-facing services and is contested for pure B2B SaaS; any customer-facing Luminara surface (portals, forms, quote acceptance) is the likelier target, and public-sector buyers will require EN 301 549 contractually ([Karl Groves](https://karlgroves.com/the-european-accessibility-act-and-b2b-software-what-internal-platforms-must-comply-with/), vendor-level source).

## Twelve design principles for Luminara's agent layer

Each principle names its evidence base. Those marked (inference) go beyond the cited sources and need Luminara's own testing.

1. **Gate by object class and reversibility, deterministically.** Create-lead and move-one-lead run directly because they are internal and reversible; delete and move-many require approval. Do not let model confidence, or a model-judged review, open or close the gate ([Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/choose-how-notion-agent-asks-for-your-approval); [Smashing](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-agentic-ai-practical-ux-patterns/)).
2. **Treat the receipt as the product's spine.** Every call stores id, params, the agent's reason, and the records' state at request time, and is immutable. The receipt is the unit that the inbox, timeline, undo and the compliance export all read from ([Salesforce tracing](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/ai/agentforce/guide/otel-api.html); [GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/agents/coding-agent/about-coding-agent)).
3. **Re-check at execution and say so.** Before an approved call runs, compare current record state with the stored request-time state; on mismatch, refuse or re-propose and write the outcome to the receipt. Show "as requested / now" side by side on the approval card (inference; no researched product documents this).
4. **Label the agent's reason as an unverified claim.** Render it secondary and attributed ("Agent says"), never as system truth, never in a control path. Put it next to the action, as Smashing's rationale pattern does ([Smashing](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-agentic-ai-practical-ux-patterns/)).
5. **Show a diff, not a button.** Approval cards list each affected record with name, current stage or owner, and the exact change, with the count of records affected for bulk moves. Approving a delete should name what will be lost and state that it cannot be undone ([Smashing](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-agentic-ai-practical-ux-patterns/); [Art. 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)).
6. **Give the agent an identity, not an avatar.** Model it as an actor in timelines and owner fields, always "on behalf of" a named human, who stays accountable ([Linear agents](https://linear.app/docs/agents-in-linear.md)). Label actions by verb, and if a symbol is needed, use a function-specific one with a text label ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-sparkles-icon-problem/); [Shape of AI](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/symbols)).
7. **Let ownership move with human edits.** Agent-authored values carry a subdued mark that clears when a person edits them, and the receipt notes the transfer ([Granola help](https://help.granola.ai/article/ai-enhanced-notes)).
8. **Pull for review, push only for blocking states.** A single review inbox with status filters (Needs approval, Done, Failed) holds proposals; notify out-of-band only when work is blocked or has failed, so the agent never stalls silently ([HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/ai/review-agent-output); [Shape of AI](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/verification)).
9. **Make autonomy visible and measurable.** Show the current mode in the panel, flag any relaxation of gates, and track approval rate, edit rate and reversal rate per action type so a rising reversal rate can pull a task back down the ladder ([Smashing](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-agentic-ai-practical-ux-patterns/), illustrative thresholds only).
10. **Keep the shell stable and the chrome quiet.** Deterministic navigation, record pages and action bars; tokenised OKLCH theming from a few inputs; borders and decoration subordinate to content; no text over variable backgrounds; contrast, motion and density under user control ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/generative-ui/); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/glassmorphism/)).
11. **Disclose AI at first interaction and carry machine-readable provenance.** A persistent "AI" label in the assistant panel (not only in terms), the assistant stating that it is AI when asked, and fields on agent-touched records for actor type, model, run id, timestamp and approval state, exportable to support customers' own labelling and privacy-policy duties ([Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/data-privacy-and-cyber-risk/ai-transparency-european-commissions-guidelines-on-article-50-part-1-provider-obligations); [Allens](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2026/10/mandatory-automated-decision-making-disclosure-requirements-are-coming-key-takeaways-from-the-oaic-guidance/)).
12. **Build to WCAG 2.2 AA now; cross-check with APCA; add a stop control.** Approve, reject and Stop buttons at least 24px (ideally larger), status messages through polite live regions without per-token announcements, sticky composers that never obscure focus, click alternatives to any drag, predictable focus return on panel close, and a global and per-run Stop reachable by keyboard ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/); [W3C WCAG 3](https://w3.org/TR/WCAG3/); the live-region mapping is inference).

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "how do we look less like AI" to "where does the evidence live." Interfaces are getting cheaper to produce, so generic polish is no longer a signal, while receipts, diffs, provenance and re-checks cannot be faked by a template. That makes Luminara's receipt-and-recheck design a craft statement as much as a safety feature: it is the part of the product that a model-generated dashboard would not contain unless someone decided it mattered. The visual layer should follow the same discipline of subtraction that Linear applies, with the agent shown through verbs, named identity and attributed claims instead of sparkle or persona.

Three uncertainties remain open and should drive Luminara's own testing before 2030. First, no controlled evidence exists on approval fatigue or automation bias in agent UIs, so approval rates and edit rates need to be instrumented from day one. Second, whether assistant-drafted B2B text falls under Art. 50(2), and how far the Australian ADM duty reaches lead prioritisation, are unresolved in the sources, which argues for exporting provenance and decision metadata rather than guessing at the boundary. Third, the 2030 durability ranking of design traits (conventions, user-controlled contrast and command surfaces over glass, free-form generated layouts and spatial surfaces) is a synthesis from legibility failures and NN/g's learnability argument, not a measured result.
